# Aria and the Very Important Hello
### Aria's Adventure Club, Book 1. Prose version, draft 2 (with read-aloud refrains)

Aria's age: 6 months. Bahamian/British spelling. About 1,050 words across 14 spreads plus a final page (standard 32-page book).

**Refrains**
1. **The wave** (spreads 3, 10, final page): "Up went her arm. / Flap went her fingers. / SWOOP went her hand."
2. **The town chant** (spreads 5, 6, broken on 7, transformed on 14): "Good morning here, good morning there, / good mornings, mornings everywhere!"
3. **The kick** (spreads 1, 11, final page): "Kick. Kick. KICK-KICK-KICK."

Recurring "find it" details: the neighbourhood potcake (follows them all over town) and a small yellow butterfly (absent when the text says so, present when it does not).

---

**Spread 1**

Every morning, Mama leaned over the crib and said the same thing.

"Good morning, sunshine."

And every morning, Aria said hello back. Not with words. Aria was six months old, and words were still a long way off.

She said it with her legs.

Kick. Kick. KICK-KICK-KICK.

Mama said it was the loudest quiet hello she had ever heard.

*Art: Jalousie windows, early light, Aria's legs a happy blur.*

---

**Spread 2**

Aria had lots of hellos.

When Grandma came up the front steps, she squealed so high that the potcake by the gate flattened his ears.

When the potcake stared at her through the screen door, she stared right back. That was a hello too. Probably.

And when she saw the baby in the mirror, she leaned in close and said, "BAH!"

The baby in the mirror said "BAH!" right back.

Aria thought that was very good manners.

---

**Spread 3**

Lately, Aria had been working on a brand-new hello.

A wave.

It went like this:

Up went her arm.
Flap went her fingers.
SWOOP went her hand.

It looked as if she were trying to catch a butterfly.

There was no butterfly.

"She's waving!" said Ms. Shay.

"She's swatting," said Mama.

Aria did it again, just to be sure.

*Art: No butterfly anywhere. Ms. Shay delighted, Mama trying not to laugh. Three small panels could stage the arm, the flap and the swoop.*

---

**Spread 4**

One Tuesday morning, Mama had errands. The light bill needed paying. They were out of bread. And there was a letter to post.

So Aria rode into town in her striped stroller, with the sunshade pulled halfway down so she could still see out. (She had insisted. Loudly.)

Behind them trotted the potcake, as if he had errands too.

*Art: Painted wooden houses, a yellow elder in bloom, a corner shop with hand-lettered signs. Ordinary, lived-in town.*

---

**Spread 5**

Town was full of people, and Mama greeted every single one.

"Morning, Mr. Rolle!"

Mr. Rolle stopped sweeping outside his shop and leaned on his broom. "Mornin', mornin'! And mornin' to the little lady."

"Good morning, Miss Pearl!"

Miss Pearl looked up from her mangoes. "Look at that baby, gettin' so big!"

Good morning here, good morning there,
good mornings, mornings everywhere!

---

**Spread 6**

Mama said good morning to the lady at the bakery counter. She said good morning to the jitney driver at the light. She said good morning to a man in a car who wasn't even looking.

He looked then. He said good morning back.

Aria's head went left. Aria's head went right.

Good morning here, good morning there,
good mornings, mornings everywhere!

Some were loud. Some were just a nod. One was two fingers lifted off a steering wheel.

Aria had not known there were so many hellos in the whole world.

*Art: Aria leaning out of the stroller, mouth a perfect O, surrounded by small greeting moments.*

---

**Spread 7**

Good morning here, good morning there...

But outside the bank, there was a bench, and on the bench sat a boy who did not look like good morning at all.

He wore a crisp white shirt, shiny new shoes, and a backpack so big it looked as if it might carry him off. His aunt sat beside him, checking the time.

Everybody else in town was moving. The boy was not. He sat very still and very small, holding his backpack straps with both hands.

Aria stopped looking left and right. She looked at him.

The boy looked at Aria.

Then he looked at his shoes.

*Art: The town bustles in every direction. Kai is the only still thing on the page.*

---

**Spread 8**

"Morning," said Mama.

"Morning," said the aunt. "This is Kai. New school today. We're waiting on his ride."

"And this is Aria," said Mama.

Kai didn't say anything. He pressed his new shoes together, heel to heel.

"New school," said Mama. "That's a big day."

---

**Spread 9**

Aria didn't know what school was. She didn't know about teachers or bells or lunch kits.

But she knew about new.

New could be wonderful, like the first time she tasted mashed-up pawpaw.

New could also be too much, like the lady at church who pinched her cheeks. Aria had pressed her face into Mama's neck and stayed there until the lady went away.

Kai looked like the second kind of new.

*Art: Two soft-edged memory vignettes: pawpaw delight, and Aria hiding in Mama's neck.*

---

**Spread 10**

Kai peeked at Aria again.

So Aria gave him her best smile. It was very wide and very gummy and had no teeth in it at all.

Kai's mouth twitched.

Then Aria decided it was time.

Up went her arm.
Flap went her fingers.
SWOOP went her hand.

It was the best wave she had ever done.

It was still mostly a swat.

*Art: This time there IS a butterfly, small and yellow, just above her hand. Nobody notices but the reader.*

---

**Spread 11**

Kai stared at her hand.

Then, slowly, he let go of one backpack strap and lifted his own.

He waved back.

And his face did something it had not done all morning. It smiled.

He slid off the bench and came over to the stroller. "Hi, Aria," he said.

Kick. Kick. KICK-KICK-KICK.

Aria flapped both arms and announced, "GAH-BAH-BLAAAH-DEH!"

Kai laughed. His aunt laughed. Mama laughed. Even Mr. Rolle, across the street, laughed, and he had no idea what was funny.

Aria laughed hardest of all, because everyone else was laughing and it seemed like the right thing to do.

---

**Spread 12**

Then a car horn went beep-beep.

A silver car pulled up to the kerb. A lady waved from the driver's seat. The back door swung open.

Kai's laugh stopped. His smile went somewhere far away. He stood up, and then he stopped, holding both straps again.

His feet would not go.

Nobody told Kai to be brave. Nobody told him there was nothing to worry about.

"New things can feel big," Mama said quietly.

Kai's aunt put her hand on his back and left it there.

---

**Spread 13**

In the back seat, a girl in the same white shirt looked out at Kai. She pulled her bag off the seat and dropped it on the floor. Then she patted the empty space.

Pat, pat.

Kai looked at his aunt.

He looked at the car.

He looked back at Aria.

Aria was still waving. She had been waving the whole time. By now she was waving with both arms and kicking with both feet, which is the biggest hello a person can do without standing up.

Kai lifted his hand one more time. "Bye, Aria."

Then he climbed in beside the girl, and the door closed.

His tummy was still flip-flopping. You could see it in his face at the window.

But he went.

---

**Spread 14**

On the way home, the potcake trotted ahead and Mama walked slowly.

"You know what, Miss Aria?" she said. "That was a very important hello."

Aria didn't know what important meant. She was busy chewing her stroller strap.

But she had heard a lot of hellos that day.

Hellos here, hellos there,
hellos, hellos everywhere.

Loud ones and quiet ones. Spoken ones and nodded ones. Hellos with brooms and hellos with mangoes and a hello lifted off a steering wheel. A hello that was a pat on an empty seat.

And one that looked a lot like a swat at a butterfly.

Across town, a classroom door opened.

*Art: Kai at the door of his new classroom, the girl from the car beside him. He gives a small, careful wave to someone inside.*

---

**Final page**

The next morning, Mama leaned over the crib.

"Good morning, sunshine."

Aria looked at her. She thought about it.

Up went her arm.
Flap went her fingers.
Swoop went her hand, wobbly and slow.

Mama's whole face lit up. "Well," she said, "hello to you, too."

Kick. Kick. KICK-KICK-KICK.

Some hellos are worth saying twice.

*Art: Morning light. The yellow butterfly on the windowsill.*
