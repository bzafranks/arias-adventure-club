# Aria's Adventure Club — Series Writing Notes

Working decisions that sit alongside the Master Story & Book Series Specification. Add to this file as each book settles a new convention.

## Text format

- **Default format is prose with short repeated refrains.** Refrains give the read-aloud pull of rhyme (children join in, parents get a rhythm to perform) without forcing every page into rhyme.
- **Full rhyme is reserved** for books built on comedy, chaos or a strong repeated pattern.
- **Refrain guidelines:**
  - Two or three refrains per book at most, so each one stays recognisable.
  - Each refrain is short (one to three lines) and physical or sayable: something a child can do or chant.
  - Repeat a refrain at least three times. Change it once at a key moment (break it off at the turn, or transform it near the end) so the repetition carries meaning.
  - A refrain can rhyme even when the book does not, as with the Book 1 town chant.
- **Target length for picture books: about 600 to 850 words** of story text (art notes excluded).
- **Editing test for every book:** for each paragraph, ask "Does the child need me to say this, or can the picture show it?" Move anything the illustration can carry into the art notes.
- **When an older secondary character carries the emotional problem,** let them say their fear out loud once. The answer comes through action, never through an adult explaining it, and the narration never points out the connection.
- Contractions are fine in story text and dialogue.
- Bahamian/British spelling throughout (practised, neighbourhood, kerb, colour).

## Back matter: Aria's Adventure Journal (every book)

Every book ends with an "Aria's Adventure Journal" spread straight after the story. It is where the lesson is stated, so the story itself never has to state it.

- **What Aria Discovered:** one or two sentences naming what the story showed.
- **Talk About It:** four to six questions. Mix personal questions ("Have you ever felt nervous somewhere new?") with one or two about the story itself, so parents can check understanding (Book 1: "Who said hi to him first?").
- **Aria's Adventure Club Challenge:** one small, doable action for today, with a few concrete examples, ending on a short memorable line.
- **Look Again:** one or two prompts that send the child back into the pictures to find the book's "find it" details.
- The journal's word count is separate from the story's 600 to 850 words.
- Layout: the journal takes its own spread after the final story spread, followed by a blank page, which keeps the page count a multiple of 4 (Book 1: 36 pages).

## Family cast

- Aria's father does not appear in, and is not referenced by, any story.
- Recurring family: Mama, Grandma, Grandpa, Aunt Gail and Aunty Ann (Mama's sisters), Cousin Amy (Aunt Gail's daughter, 22; always called "Cousin Amy" in the text) and nanny Ms. Shay.
- Bring family members in only when a story needs them. Do not add them to fill a role; there are many books ahead to introduce them properly.
- Character designs live in the Character Bible files. Design each family member from reference photos before they appear in art.

## Visual storytelling

- Watercolour and ink style for all books.
- Each book carries one or two "find it" details that reward rereading (Book 1: the potcake and the yellow butterfly).
- Let the art contradict or extend the text at least once (Book 1: the narrator says there is no butterfly; the art shows one).
